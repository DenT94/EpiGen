\section*{Why this document exists}

The scoring machinery grew in two places at once (this session and a parallel
one working the same files), and the word ``score'' now refers to at least
five distinct quantities, two of which are both called \texttt{wt\_score} and
disagree with each other. This is a precise map of what each one is, where
it's computed, what it's used for, and where the definitions currently
conflict.

\tableofcontents

\section{The three underlying experts}

Every score in the pipeline is built from three independent models, each
queried on the \emph{compensatory window} (\texttt{window\_positions}) or on
a whole candidate sequence.

\begin{description}
  \item[ESM2] (\texttt{oracle/scoring.py}) --- per-position amino-acid
    log-probabilities, $P(\text{aa}_i \mid \text{sequence})$, from a single
    unmasked forward pass. This is an \emph{approximation} of the true
    masked-marginal pseudo-log-likelihood ($P(\text{aa}_i \mid$ sequence with
    position $i$ masked$)$, which would need $L$ forward passes for a
    length-$L$ sequence); the model gets to see the residue it's predicting.
    Traded deliberately for speed: 1 pass instead of $L$.
  \item[ProteinMPNN] (\texttt{oracle/scoring.py}) --- per-position
    amino-acid log-probabilities \emph{conditioned on the backbone
    structure}, $P(\text{aa}_i \mid \text{structure}, \text{context})$.
    Already single-pass (autoregressive teacher-forcing), no approximation
    needed.
  \item[Evo2] (\texttt{oracle/evo2\_scoring.py}) --- operates on DNA, not
    protein: the AA sequence is reverse-translated to nt via
    \texttt{oracle/codon.py}, and every accepted AA substitution during the
    search updates the corresponding codon. Two functions now exist here.
    \texttt{nt\_sequence\_score\_batch} gives one scalar
    \texttt{avg\_log\_likelihood} per \emph{whole nucleotide sequence}
    (causal: mean of $P(x_t \mid x_{<t})$ over the sequence) --- this was,
    until this revision, the only thing Evo2 contributed to
    \texttt{combined\_score}. \texttt{window\_log\_prob\_batch} (new) instead
    requests \texttt{Evo2ScoringConfig.return\_logits=True} --- a
    \texttt{(seq\_len, vocab\_size)} per-nucleotide table, the same shape
    ESM2/ProteinMPNN already return --- and sums only the log-probability
    Evo2 assigned to the nucleotide actually present at each codon of the
    window's AA positions. \texttt{combined\_score} (Section~\ref{sec:combined})
    now uses this window-restricted version.
\end{description}

Both ESM2 and ProteinMPNN expose \texttt{\_batch} variants that score many
sequences in a single Modal call --- this is what lets the MCMC round loop
recompute every chain's proposal in one call per expert per round, instead of
one call per chain.

\section{\texttt{window\_score}: the shared per-position formula}
\label{sec:window}

\texttt{oracle/mcmc.py}'s \texttt{window\_score} is the one formula almost
everything else is built from:

\begin{equation}
  \texttt{window\_score}(s) = \sum_{p \,\in\, \text{window\_positions}}
    \Big[ w_{\text{esm2}} \cdot \text{ESM2}(p, s_p) \;+\; w_{\text{pmpnn}} \cdot \text{MPNN}(p, s_p) \Big]
\end{equation}

where $s_p$ is the amino acid sequence $s$ has at position $p$, and
$\text{ESM2}(p, a)$ / $\text{MPNN}(p, a)$ are the two experts' precomputed
per-position log-probability tables for amino acid $a$ at position $p$.

Two properties matter:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{It only sums over \texttt{window\_positions}}, never the
    whole sequence. This is intentional, not a shortcut: the fixed edit and
    every other unchanged residue contribute an identical constant to every
    candidate's score, so they cancel out of any accept/reject comparison
    and are simply omitted.
  \item \textbf{Evo2 is not literally inside this function}, but (as of this
    revision) it gets the equivalent window-restricted treatment via
    \texttt{oracle.evo2\_scoring.window\_log\_prob\_batch}, added as a
    separate term wherever the full search objective is needed
    (Section~\ref{sec:combined}) rather than merged into this function's own
    per-AA-position dict.
\end{itemize}

\texttt{window\_score} is a \emph{public} function specifically so a caller
holding its own \texttt{esm2\_scores}/\texttt{pmpnn\_scores} tables (e.g.\
ones already spent on the oracle sanity checks, Section~\ref{sec:sanity}) can
score an arbitrary sequence --- WT, a chain's starting point, a chain's
ending point --- at zero extra Modal cost, using the ESM2+ProteinMPNN portion
of what the search itself optimizes.

\paragraph{Why isn't Evo2 merged directly into \texttt{window\_score}?}
Two reasons, both still true even now that Evo2's contribution is
window-restricted. First, a \emph{vocabulary} mismatch: Evo2's logits are
over its nucleotide vocabulary, not the 20-AA \texttt{CANONICAL\_AA}
vocabulary \texttt{window\_score} sums over --- \texttt{window\_log\_prob\_batch}
aggregates each codon's 3 nucleotide log-probs into one per-AA-position value
via \texttt{oracle/codon.py}'s AA-position $\leftrightarrow$ codon-triplet
mapping, but that's a different-shaped computation from a lookup into a
plain per-AA table, not a rename. Second, and more fundamentally, Evo2 is
\emph{autoregressive}: its logit at position $t$ is conditioned on
everything before $t$, unlike ESM2's single-pass approximation or
ProteinMPNN's structure-conditioning, neither of which has that directional
dependency. A table computed once on \texttt{edit\_only} would be
systematically wrong for any candidate that differs from \texttt{edit\_only}
\emph{upstream} of a given window position --- so unlike
\texttt{esm2\_scores}/\texttt{pmpnn\_scores}, there is no single cached Evo2
table a caller could reuse for free across arbitrary sequences the way this
function's other two arguments already are. \texttt{window\_log\_prob\_batch}
is instead called fresh per batch of actual candidates, exactly like
\texttt{nt\_sequence\_score\_batch} already was --- same call pattern, no
new Modal cost, just a different sum over the same returned data.

\section{\texttt{combined\_score}: what the search actually optimizes}
\label{sec:combined}

The number shown in the ``MCMC candidates'' table
(\texttt{MCMCCandidate.combined\_score}) and the number the round loop
accepts/rejects on is:

\begin{equation}
  \texttt{combined\_score}(s) = \texttt{window\_score}(s) \;+\; w_{\text{evo2}} \cdot \texttt{window\_log\_prob\_batch}(s)
\end{equation}

The Evo2 term is only computed (and only added) when Evo2 scoring is
enabled, i.e.\ when a nucleotide sequence was supplied
(\texttt{orchestrate.py}'s \texttt{use\_evo2}, \textbf{default
\texttt{True}}). When Evo2 is off, \texttt{combined\_score} \emph{is}
\texttt{window\_score} --- the two coincide, which is presumably why it
looked from the outside like ``the score'' was always just ESM2+ProteinMPNN.
With Evo2 on (the default), they are not the same number.

\textbf{As of this revision}, all three experts contributing to
\texttt{combined\_score} are genuinely window-restricted --- before, Evo2's
term was a whole-nt-sequence average that diluted the window-position signal
with every unchanged position elsewhere in the sequence (see
Section~\ref{sec:window}'s ``Why isn't Evo2 merged directly'' note for how
this was fixed). This makes \texttt{combined\_score} more internally
consistent than it was, but does \emph{not} by itself resolve the
\texttt{wt\_score} collision in Section~\ref{sec:collision} --- the search's
own \texttt{wt\_score} still numerically differs from
\texttt{EndToEndResult.wt\_score} whenever Evo2 is on, since the latter is
still deliberately Evo2-free (Section~\ref{sec:collision} explains why: no
free, reusable per-sequence Evo2 table exists for the same reason
\texttt{window\_score} can't merge Evo2 in directly).

\warnbox{Every docstring/UI caption I wrote for the results-page histogram
says ``ESM2+ProteinMPNN, Evo2 excluded'' --- that's accurate for what the
histogram plots, but it made it look like Evo2 wasn't part of the pipeline at
all, or that it categorically \emph{couldn't} be scored per-position. Neither
is true: it \emph{is} part of \texttt{combined\_score} by default, and (as of
this revision) genuinely window-restricted there too. Only the free,
no-extra-Modal-call comparison plot excludes it, because there's no single
cached table to reuse across sequences the way \texttt{esm2\_scores}/
\texttt{pmpnn\_scores} are reused.}

\section{The oracle sanity checks: \texttt{expert\_correlation} and \texttt{fraction\_below\_wt}}
\label{sec:sanity}

Computed once, before MCMC starts, in \texttt{oracle/correlation.py}. These
are diagnostics on the \emph{full substitution space} at the window
positions --- every possible single-residue swap, not sequences the search
actually visited.

\begin{description}
  \item[\texttt{expert\_agreement}] Pearson correlation between ESM2's and
    ProteinMPNN's per-(position, amino acid) log-likelihood \emph{deltas
    versus the reference residue's own score} at that position. Low
    correlation is the desired outcome --- it means the two experts are
    contributing independent information, not both re-deriving the same
    signal (per \texttt{mypipelinethoughts.md} step 4).
  \item[\texttt{fraction\_below\_wt}] Fraction of (position, amino acid)
    substitutions whose $w_{\text{esm2}}\text{ESM2} + w_{\text{pmpnn}}\text{MPNN}$
    combined score is \emph{below} the reference residue's own combined
    score at that position. A sanity check that the window isn't
    uniformly hostile to substitution before trusting any candidate MCMC
    finds.
\end{description}

\warnbox{Both functions take a parameter literally named \texttt{wt\_sequence},
but \texttt{orchestrate.py} always calls them with
\texttt{edit\_only.sequence}, never true WT. The parameter name means ``the
reference sequence this comparison is relative to,'' not literal wild-type
--- same naming looseness that caused the \texttt{mutation\_name} ``WT''
mislabeling bug fixed earlier this session (Section~\ref{sec:naming}).}

\section{\texorpdfstring{The \texttt{wt\_score} collision}{The wt\_score collision}}
\label{sec:collision}

This is the actual source of the confusion. \textbf{Two different
quantities are both currently called \texttt{wt\_score}}, computed in two
different files, by two different sessions, for two different purposes:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.24\linewidth}p{0.36\linewidth}p{0.36\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
 & \texttt{MCMCSearchResult.wt\_score} & \texttt{EndToEndResult.wt\_score} \\
\midrule
\textbf{Where computed} & \texttt{oracle/mcmc.py}, inside \texttt{run\_mcmc\_search}, at round 0 & \texttt{orchestrate.py}, separately, after the search returns \\
\textbf{Formula} & \texttt{combined\_score} of the unmutated starting chain (\texttt{window\_score + w\_evo2*window\_log\_prob\_batch}, window-restricted, if Evo2 enabled) & \texttt{window\_score} only, hardcoded weights $0.5/0.5$, \textbf{no Evo2 term ever} \\
\textbf{Used for} & Per-chain \emph{freeze threshold}: a chain stops proposing further mutations once its score exceeds this (early stopping / convergence) & The dashed vertical reference line on the results-page starting/ending-score histogram \\
\textbf{Currently wired to UI?} & No --- \texttt{orchestrate.py} does not read this field at all & Yes, this is what \texttt{plot\_score\_comparison} actually draws \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{These are different numbers whenever Evo2 is enabled} (the default).
\texttt{orchestrate.py} currently ignores the search's own \texttt{wt\_score}
/ \texttt{rounds\_run} / \texttt{converged\_chain\_count} fields entirely and
recomputes a narrower version itself from scratch, using the
\texttt{esm2\_scores}/\texttt{pmpnn\_scores} tables it already has lying
around from Section~\ref{sec:sanity}'s sanity checks.

\subsection*{Why it happened}

Two sessions extended \texttt{run\_mcmc\_search} independently on the same
underlying idea (``compare against the WT/edit-only baseline'') for two
different reasons: I added \texttt{starting\_sequences}/
\texttt{ending\_sequences} plus a post-hoc, Modal-call-free comparison plot
for the results page; the other session added early-stopping (freeze a chain
once it beats the unmutated baseline) directly inside the search loop, which
needed its own baseline computed \emph{during} the search, with the full
objective it's actually optimizing, not just the window-only piece I use for
the free comparison.

\subsection*{What this means practically right now}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The results-page histogram's WT dashed line is \textbf{narrower}
    than what the search itself used to decide convergence, whenever Evo2 is
    on. A chain can legitimately be ``frozen'' (converged, per the search's
    own \texttt{wt\_score}) while still plotting on the wrong side of the
    histogram's dashed line (per the narrower \texttt{window\_score}-only
    baseline).
  \item \texttt{rounds\_run} and \texttt{converged\_chain\_count} --- real,
    useful information about whether the search actually finished early or
    ran the full \texttt{steps} --- aren't surfaced anywhere in the UI yet.
  \item Nothing is silently \emph{wrong} in the sense of a crash or a raised
    exception; the two numbers are each internally consistent for their own
    purpose. They're just both named \texttt{wt\_score}, which is the actual
    bug.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Options going forward (not yet decided)}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Unify on the search's own \texttt{wt\_score}.} Drop
    orchestrate.py's separate computation; use
    \texttt{mcmc\_result.wt\_score} (and \texttt{chain\_starting\_scores}/
    \texttt{ending\_scores} recomputed with the Evo2 term included, at the
    cost of one extra Evo2 call per chain start/end) for the histogram too.
    Makes the plot match \texttt{combined\_score} exactly; costs more Modal
    calls.
  \item \textbf{Keep both, rename one.} E.g.\ rename
    \texttt{EndToEndResult.wt\_score} to \texttt{wt\_window\_score} to make
    the scope explicit everywhere it's displayed, and leave it
    intentionally Evo2-free (cheap, no extra Modal calls). Cheapest fix,
    doesn't reconcile the actual numeric disagreement, just stops it being
    mislabeled.
  \item \textbf{Surface both, explained.} Show \texttt{rounds\_run} /
    \texttt{converged\_chain\_count} on the results page, and label the
    histogram's baseline explicitly as ``ESM2+ProteinMPNN only, Evo2
    excluded'' directly on the chart, not just in a caption.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Naming: what ``WT'' means at each call site}
\label{sec:naming}

A recurring, related source of confusion: the string ``WT'' and the
parameter name \texttt{wt\_sequence} get reused loosely throughout the
codebase to mean ``the reference/baseline sequence for this particular
comparison,'' which is \emph{usually} \texttt{edit\_only.sequence} (WT with
the fixed edit already applied), not literal wild-type.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.42\linewidth}p{0.58\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
Call site & What ``reference'' actually means there \\
\midrule
\texttt{correlation.expert\_agreement}/\texttt{fraction\_below\_wt} & \texttt{edit\_only.sequence} \\
\texttt{mcmc.run\_mcmc\_search}'s internal \texttt{wt\_score} & \texttt{folded.sequence}, which \texttt{orchestrate.py} always passes as \texttt{edit\_only} \\
\texttt{naming.mutation\_name} (now fixed) & Caller-supplied; \texttt{results.py}/\texttt{structure.py} now correctly pass true \texttt{wt\_sequence}, after a bug where they passed \texttt{edit\_only.sequence} and a candidate identical to it rendered as the string \texttt{"WT"} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The \texttt{mutation\_name} case was an actual shipped bug (fixed this
session): an MCMC candidate whose trajectory never found an accepted
compensatory move is identical to \texttt{edit\_only}, not to true WT --- it
still carries the fixed edit. Labeling it \texttt{"WT"} claimed something
false. It now names against true \texttt{wt\_sequence}, so such a candidate
renders as just the edit itself (e.g.\ \texttt{M1A}) instead of a misleading
\texttt{"WT"}.

\section{A fourth, unrelated ``score'': SAE $\Delta\Delta$}

Worth naming explicitly so it doesn't get folded into the above by accident:
the SAE feature deltas (\texttt{sae\_diff/run.py}, surfaced in the Structure
viewer's ``Color by $\Delta\Delta$SAE feature'' dropdowns and the results
page's PCA scatter) are \textbf{not} an oracle score at all. They're
interpretability feature \emph{activations} from ESM~C's sparse autoencoder
--- a completely different model, a completely different notion of
``score,'' with no relationship to \texttt{window\_score}/
\texttt{combined\_score} beyond happening to also be a number attached to a
candidate sequence. Don't try to compare a $\Delta\Delta$SAE value against a
\texttt{combined\_score} value; they're not on the same scale or measuring
the same thing.
